Finlayson Caribou and the KZK Mine Project

1 Introduction

The workbook contains a summary of information sources on the Finlayson Caribou Herd, especially in the context of the Kudz Ze Kayah mine project. In this section, we review the findings of the 2020 report. The other sections provide technical summaries of population estimates, seasonal ranges, and habitat suitability in and around the project area.

1.1 KZK Screen Report: Finlayson caribou herd

The section regarding the Finlayson caribou herd (FCH) in the Screening Report identifies the Kudz Ze Kayah Project as a source of significant adverse effects to the herd, primarily through habitat displacement and interference with seasonal movements.

1.1.1 Summary of the FCH Assessment

  • Cultural and Ecological Importance: Caribou are a “cultural keystone species” for the Kaska Dena, as reflected in the project’s name, Kudz Ze Kayah, meaning “land of the caribou”. They are also an umbrella species, meaning protections for them benefit the broader ecosystem.
  • Current Status: The FCH is a sub-type of woodland caribou designated as a “population of special concern”. The herd is in a persistent decline, dropping approximately 12% between 2007 and 2017 to an estimated 2,700 animals.
  • Project Impacts:
    • Habitat Loss: The project is uniquely situated at the intersection of late winter, calving, post-calving, rut, and movement habitats. It will remove approximately 5% of high-quality rutting habitat and 4% of post-calving habitat.
    • Movement Barriers: The project area is a “caribou crossing” and a portal to critical calving areas; disruptions here affect a significant proportion of the population.
    • Increased Predation: Clearing the access road in winter facilitates easier movement for wolves, likely increasing predation on caribou.
  • Recommended Mitigations: The report recommends establishing an oversight body with First Nations representation to manage caribou-specific mitigations adaptively. It also requires the Government of Yukon to develop a comprehensive long-term range management plan for the herd.

1.1.2 Critique of the FCH Section

The report offers a rigorous assessment but highlights several critical information gaps and methodological tensions:

  • Reliance on Poor Data: The Executive Committee (EC) explicitly notes that available information regarding the FCH is “poor” compared to other Yukon herds. There have been no collaring efforts since the 1980s, and there is a significant lack of data regarding adult mortality.
  • Unmodeled Impacts: While some habitats were quantified, the EC notes that effects on calving habitat and movement corridors were not modeled, which they describe as a “significant knowledge gap”. This suggests the actual consequences to the population might be higher than the report’s statistics indicate.
  • Conservative “Zone of Influence”: The Proponent applied a 3 km zone of influence for their models, which the EC notes is on the “lower end” of estimates seen in scientific literature; other studies have shown caribou avoiding mines by up to 23 km.
  • Conflict Over Significance: There is a clear divergence between the Proponent and the EC. The Proponent argued that because the “human footprint” in the range is only 12%, the project’s magnitude of effect should be considered “low”. The EC countered that caribou may be “naive” to the risks of new industrial footprints, and the herd’s continued decline warrants high caution regardless of the total percentage of disturbed land.
  • Uncertainty of Reversibility: While the Proponent suggests effects are reversible through reclamation, the EC points out that the effectiveness of many mitigations during operations is largely unknown, and the loss of the open pit and waste storage facilities is permanent and irreversible.

Ultimately, the report concludes that if the project proceeds, the FCH population will likely be lower than it would have been otherwise, placing the herd’s long-term sustainability at risk.
